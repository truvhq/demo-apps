Embedding Truv Orders in benefits applications

Capturing income, or verifying a declared figure

Two different integrations, not two settings of one. They sit at different points in the eligibility workflow and ask different things of the applicant. Decide which you are building before you design the screen.

Flow A · Recommended

Capture and verify

Truv collects the income. The applicant never types a figure.

The applicant reaches the income step and connects their employer or their bank. The verified income, employer, pay rate, pay frequency and pay statements come back on the order. What you do with them is yours to decide: populate your own form, or leave the review to Truv and read the result.

There is nothing for the applicant to remember, no paystub to find, and no arithmetic. For the income Truv retrieves there is also no attested number to reconcile, because the attestation and the verification are the same event.

Choose it when you are building or substantially revising an application flow and you control the income step.

It does not remove the other paths. "No reconciliation" holds only for the income Truv actually retrieves. An applicant whose income Truv cannot reach still needs the document-upload route or manual entry, so build both.

Where self-certification is on, what the applicant confirms is a declared figure, not a computed countable-income one. Deriving countable monthly income stays with the agency, under its own rules.

Flow B

Verify attested income

The applicant states their income. Truv produces the evidence.

The applicant declares income in your existing form - or already did, on a renewal - and Truv runs against the same person to retrieve what the payroll provider or bank reports. Your eligibility rules compare the two.

Choose it when you cannot change the order of your existing application, when you are verifying at renewal against income already on file, or when your eligibility engine is built around comparing a declared figure to a data-source figure.

That comparison is your reasonable-compatibility check. What counts as compatible, and whether Truv is named as a data source, is set in the agency's verification plan - the agency's policy and legal teams own that, not Truv.

Letting Truv collect the attestation

Self-certification is a setting on a customization template. Pass that template_id when you create the order and a review step appears inside the embedded flow: the applicant sees each record Truv retrieved and either confirms it as representative or flags a discrepancy with a reason. Create the order against a template without it and no review step appears.

That gives you one record that is both verified and attested, which is usually what the eligibility file needs, and it surfaces a disagreement while the applicant is looking at the data rather than later, in a request for information.

Reading the result

Add the certification query parameter to the order read. It takes two values:

RequestWhat comes back
?certification=certifiedOnly the records safe to rely on. Disputed ones are dropped, along with any employer or account left empty as a result.
?certification=allEvery record, including the disputed ones and the reason the applicant gave.
 
curl --request GET \
     --url 'https://prod.truv.com/v1/orders/{order_id}/?certification=certified' \
     --header 'X-Access-Client-Id: YOUR_TRUV_CLIENT_ID' \
     --header 'X-Access-Secret: YOUR_TRUV_ACCESS_SECRET' \
     --header 'Accept: application/json'

In the response, each certified record carries a certification object alongside the verified data. That object holds the applicant's decision - representative, not_representative, pending or not_applicable - a rejection_reason where they disputed it, and certified_data, which is the snapshot of what they confirmed.

If it is on, do not build your own review screen

This is the decision the setting really makes. With self-certification on, the applicant has already reviewed and confirmed every record inside Truv, so a review screen of your own asks them to confirm the same thing twice. Read the certified records and move them on.

If you would rather own that experience, leave self-certification off and work from the verified fields yourself. Either is fine. Running both is what to avoid.

Choosing between them

You can run both. The usual split is Flow A for new applications and Flow B at renewal, where a figure is already on file. What you should not do is run Flow A and then still make the applicant type the same income into a form. See Rule 9.

Where Truv goes in the journey

At the point where your application asks about income or resources. Not before it, not on a separate page about verification, and not after the applicant has already typed a number.

Three placement decisions

Place it at the step, not in front of the step. The order landing page is already a decision screen with its own heading, its own explanation, and its own call to action. When the applicant clicks Income, that landing page should be what they see. Anything between the step and the landing page is a screen the applicant has to get through to reach a screen.

Create the order at the moment you know who it is for. Income belongs to a person, so the order does too. Pair order creation with your household-member selection, whatever form that takes in your flow, rather than creating one for the household and sorting out who it covers later.

Hand back to a screen they recognize. When the order completes, land the applicant on the next section of your application with the income step ticked. Skip the confirmation page of your own - see Rule 9.

The identity model

Get these two the wrong way round and a household arrives as one person.

FieldMaps an order toValue
external_user_idan individualYour id for that person, and the same value again on their next order
order_numbera householdWhatever identifier groups that household in your system, repeated on each member's order

One order per adult, a unique external_user_id for each, and the same order_number across the household. order_number does not have to be a case number - it is whatever value groups those people together on your side, and it is what lets you pull the household's orders back as a set.

Current behavior

Orders sharing an external_user_id resolve to one Truv user. On that profile the first name written is kept, and a later order's first_name, last_name, email and phone do not replace it. A later ssn does. Share the value across a household and the profile ends up holding one member's name next to another member's SSN.

The easier mistake is omitting it. With no external_user_id, the API falls back to order_number for backwards compatibility. Since order_number is the same for everyone in the household, leaving external_user_id unset puts them all on one Truv user - without anyone having reused an identifier.

Reusing external_user_id for the same person is the point, not a side effect. On their next order the landing page already shows the employer they connected last time, which is what makes a renewal short.

It also matters when someone picks up another job. A new order against the same external_user_id sits with the connections that person already has, so they are adding an employer to a record rather than starting again as a stranger.

Pass the employer you already know

What you pass at order creation decides how much searching the applicant has to do. Passing an employer removes two screens from their path, and if you already hold the name there is no case where you are better off withholding it.

What passing an employer does to the order

Whether you pass employers, and whether the applicant may add their own, gives you three shapes. Both behaviors are configured with us, so tell us which one you want rather than sending it on a request.

ShapeYou passThe applicant can
Openno employerssearch for and connect whoever they work for
Semi-openthe employers you knowconnect those, and add others they also work for
Closedthe employers you knowconnect only those

Semi-open is the usual choice for a benefits application: you save the applicant the search for the job you already know about, without assuming it is their only one. The template field behind it is allow_additional_connections, and how hard the manual route is to reach is the setting in Rule 4.

If you hold the employer but do not ask for it

Send what you have as a string in company_name. Truv matches it to an employer and its payroll provider. This is the common case: the employer is on the case file or came from a previous verification, and nobody is typing anything.

curl --request POST \
     --url https://prod.truv.com/v1/orders/ \
     --header 'X-Access-Client-Id: YOUR_TRUV_CLIENT_ID' \
     --header 'X-Access-Secret: YOUR_TRUV_ACCESS_SECRET' \
     --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \
     --data '{
  "products": ["income"],
  "first_name": "Jordan",
  "last_name": "Reyes",
  "external_user_id": "household-member-4812",
  "employers": [
    { "company_name": "Home Depot" }
  ]
}'

The order page then lists that employer, and the connect button takes the applicant straight to the provider's sign-in. No search screen, no provider picker.

A name we do not recognize is still worth sending

If the name does not match anything on file, the order is still created and the name still travels with it. The applicant picks their payroll provider themselves and carries on, which is where they would have started anyway.

Those names also feed back to us. An employer we keep seeing gets added once we can confirm it is real, and mapped to a payroll provider once we can confirm which one. So sending mom-and-pop-shop.co costs the applicant nothing today and makes the next applicant's path shorter.

If your form already asks for the employer

Then do not just forward what they typed. Wire the field to Truv's company search as they type and let them pick from the results.

curl --request GET \
     --url 'https://prod.truv.com/v1/company-mappings-search/?query=Home%20Dep&product_type=income' \
     --header 'X-Access-Client-Id: YOUR_TRUV_CLIENT_ID' \
     --header 'X-Access-Secret: YOUR_TRUV_ACCESS_SECRET' \
     --header 'Accept: application/json'
[
  {
    "company_mapping_id": "b79c217de05e43938b66a09d87ff6ac0",
    "name": "Home Depot",
    "domain": "homedepot.com",
    "logo_url": "https:///company_mapping_logo/450545e3….svg",
    "success_rate": "high",
    "confidence_level": "0.9"
  }
]

Render the results as a picklist under the input. logo_url gives each row a recognizable mark, which is the difference between typing an employer's full legal name and typing three letters and clicking the logo. The search matches on names, domains and synonyms, and it absorbs a single slip - Home Dpeot and Walmrat both resolve. It is not a spellchecker: two transposed letters in one query can return nothing, so keep the input editable and do not gate submission on a match.

Watch for rows that look identical. A query can return two results with the same name and different company_mapping_id values, so show domain alongside the name or the applicant is choosing between two identical-looking rows.

When they pick one, keep its company_mapping_id on your record and pass that on every order you create for them afterwards. An id is an exact match where a name is a best-effort one, which is the whole reason to collect it.

Each result also carries a success_rate of low, high or unsupported. Use it to order your suggestions, or to set expectations before an applicant picks an employer we cannot reach.

None of this is required. Plenty of integrations send company_name and never touch the search endpoint.

Worth knowing

An order takes up to five employers.

company_mapping_id is not echoed back on the order read. Once a connection exists you can recover it: take employers[].link_id off the order, then read GET /v1/links/{link_id}/, where company_mapping carries the employer Truv resolved to. That is also the stable employer identifier to key your own records on.

This matters more in Flow B than in Flow A

In Flow B the applicant has already declared their employer, or it is on file from a previous renewal, so the name is sitting there waiting to be passed. Deeplinking is close to free and should be the default.

In Flow A you are usually capturing employment for the first time and have nothing to deeplink with. The exception is a returning applicant: reuse their external_user_id and the order page shows the employer they connected last time without your passing anything at all.

Reinitializing an order, or creating a new one

Applicants leave and come back. What your income step does on their second visit decides whether they resume or start over.

Read the order back instead of recreating it

bridge_token is returned twice: once on POST /v1/orders/ when you create the order, and again on GET /v1/orders/{order_id}/ every time you read it. It is the same token both times. When the applicant returns, read the order and mount Bridge on the token that response gives you.

Take the token from the top level

An order read carries more than one bridge_token: one at the top level, and another inside each entry of employers[] and financial_accounts[]. You want the top-level one. Mounting a suborder's token renders Unexpected Error in the container, with no exception, no console warning and a normal LOAD event, so it reads as a bad token or a broken integration. The suborder tokens are easy to reach for, because Rule 8 has you walking those same entries to read each one's status.

On your server

Read the order the member already has. If it is still open, hand its bridge_token back to the browser and they pick up where they stopped. If it is not, create a new order and hand back that token instead. The browser gets a token either way and does not need to know which happened.

curl --request GET \
     --url https://prod.truv.com/v1/orders/{order_id}/ \
     --header 'X-Access-Client-Id: YOUR_TRUV_CLIENT_ID' \
     --header 'X-Access-Secret: YOUR_TRUV_ACCESS_SECRET' \
     --header 'Accept: application/json'

An order is still open when it has not expired, been completed or been canceled. Reuse it. If any of the three are true, create a new one on the same external_user_id and store the new id against your member record.

const open = order.is_expired === false
          && order.completed_at === null
          && order.canceled_at  === null;
curl --request POST \
     --url https://prod.truv.com/v1/orders/ \
     --header 'X-Access-Client-Id: YOUR_TRUV_CLIENT_ID' \
     --header 'X-Access-Secret: YOUR_TRUV_ACCESS_SECRET' \
     --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \
     --data '{
  "products": ["income"],
  "first_name": "Jordan",
  "last_name": "Reyes",
  "external_user_id": "household-member-4812",
  "order_number": "HH-7675719",
  "notification_settings": { "suppress_user_notifications": true }
}'

In the browser - mount whatever token came back

const { bridge_token } = await fetch(`/income-step/${memberId}`).then(r => r.json());

const bridge = TruvBridge.init({
  isOrder: true,
  bridgeToken: bridge_token,
  position: { type: 'inline', container: host }
});
bridge.open();

The client code is the same either way. Resuming and starting over differ only in which token your server returns.

The order has no status field

GET /v1/orders/{order_id}/ does not return a top-level status, and neither does the order list. So this guard is always true and will remount an order the applicant already finished:

// order.status is undefined, so this never stops anything
if (order.status !== 'completed') reinitialize();

Gate on completed_at instead. status does exist on each suborder, under employers[] and financial_accounts[] alongside last_task_status - read those to see how an individual connection went, not to decide whether the order is finished.

When a member has more than one order

Each expiry leaves another order behind, all attached to the same Truv user. Truv will not pick one for you: reinitialize the most recent order that is still open and treat the rest as history.

That means you have to track which order belongs to which member. external_user_id is not returned on the order read or the order list - those carry user_id - and you cannot look an order up by it. Store the order_id and user_id against your household-member record when you create the order, and update it whenever you replace one.

They may come back on a different device

The order lives on your case, not on the browser that started it. A member who began on a library desktop and returns on a phone reaches the same order through the same server lookup, with the connection they already made still listed. Nothing needs to survive in local storage or a cookie for that to work.

It is also why the send-link button in Rule 4 is worth building. A link that reopens this order on the applicant's phone answers "not on this device" better than a manual-entry form, and the path above is what greets them when they use it.

The landing page already promises this

Truv's order page tells the applicant they can return before the link expires, and that once they finish and share they cannot. Reinitializing an open order is what they were told would happen. Remounting a completed one contradicts it.

Read the link lifetime, do not assume it

expired_at is set when the order is created, from the link-expiration setting on your account or template. It is configurable, so do not hard-code a window in your own logic - read the field.

The rules

Nine rules, each with the reason behind it, so you can weigh them against your own constraints.

About the screenshots

The rules below are illustrated with one invented application, Acme State Benefits, so that each pair differs only in the thing the rule is about. Everything outside the order page is the agency’s own screen; everything inside it is configured on the customization template.

Rule 1

Render Truv on page load. Never behind a button.

30-35% more completed verifications
Don't

Put a heading, a paragraph, and a Verify my income button on the income step and open Truv on the click.

Do

Mount the order landing page as the step's content, on arrival.

Why. Every click before the verification is a place to stop. Worse, the landing page is itself a consent-and-connect screen, so a button in front of it asks the applicant to say yes twice to the same thing.

Rule 2

Put your explainer inside the template, not above the embed.

Don't

Stack an agency heading, two reassurance paragraphs, and a what you'll need list above the embed. The landing page says all of it again, immediately below.

Do

Let the landing page carry the message, and configure that message on your customization template.

What you want to sayWhere it belongs
The step's headingorders.landing.header
Why you are asking, what happens nextorders.landing.body
The connect button's labelorders.landing.buttons.connect
The finish button's labelbuttons.review_accounts / submit_accounts
Common questionsThe landing FAQ - or orders.landing.hide_faq to drop it
The search screen headingbridge.product_settings[].texts.search_header, per product
The older bridge.texts object is marked deprecated in the API spec - use product_settings.
Which employers appear before anyone typesA custom popular-companies list, uploaded as a CSV of company name and domain, up to 15 rows
What they see on successThe success screen title and body

Your own application chrome is the exception. The progress rail, the step name and the case number orient the applicant and should stay.

The same template carries the branding

Under branding you set logo, company_name, accent_color and background_color, so the embedded page can carry the agency's mark and color rather than Truv's. There is also hide_confetti, which is worth knowing about for a benefits context, and custom_end_user_agreement if your own agreement has to appear.

What you are configuring is copy, labels, logo and color. The layout of the order page itself is Truv's, which is the trade for not having to build or maintain it. Treat the template as the surface you control and your own page as the frame around it.

Language is set per order, not per template

Pass locale when you create the order and the order renders in that language. It is a fixed list of 22 values, so if you have language-access obligations, check the list against the languages you publish in before you scope the work. Where a language is not on it, the applicant's route is your manual path, which is a reason to get Rule 4 right rather than a blocker.

Rule 3

No Yes/No gate in front of the embed.

One screen removed
Don't

Ask Does anyone in your household have income? on one screen, describe verification on a second, and mount Truv on a third.

Do

Make the income step the landing page. Keep declaration questions only where their answers drive downstream eligibility logic - then have Continue mount Bridge directly.

Rule 4

Manual entry is a link below the embed, paired with "send me the link".

The rule with the most at stake, and the one most often got wrong.

Don't

Put two equal-weight buttons above the embed - Verify automatically and Enter manually. Equal weight presents them as equally good choices, which they are not.

Do

Put one row directly beneath the embed: manual entry as a text link, and a secondary button that sends the same order to the applicant's email or phone.

Always allow manual entry. Some applicants have no connectable income source, some decline to connect one, and your own verification policy will require a self-attested route regardless. It has to exist and it has to work.

The two routes are not equally good, though. The applicant ends up hunting for paystubs, and the agency ends up with a record it cannot refresh. So place them one visual step apart: the embed owns the step, the manual route is a link below it.

Why the send-link button matters

"Not now" often means "not on this device." The applicant is on a phone at a library branch, or needs a payroll login they do not have to hand. That is a deferral, not a refusal, and the manual link is the wrong answer to it. Carrying the same order to a better moment keeps the verification route open; a manual form closes it.

Create the order with email and phone and notifications suppressed while the applicant is in your app, then un-suppress when they ask:

curl --request PATCH \
     --url https://prod.truv.com/v1/orders/{order_id}/ \
     --header 'X-Access-Client-Id: YOUR_TRUV_CLIENT_ID' \
     --header 'X-Access-Secret: YOUR_TRUV_ACCESS_SECRET' \
     --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \
     --data '{ "notification_settings": { "suppress_user_notifications": false } }'

Truv then sends the email and SMS and handles the reminder sequence. The call returns 204 with an empty body, so if you want to send the link yourself instead, read share_url from the order rather than from this response.

An escape has to leave a way forward

Both escapes mean "not here, not now", so taking one has to let the applicant move through the application. Otherwise the step has no exit: they asked for the link, they cannot verify on this device, and nothing lets them past.

Keep the embed mounted when they ask for the link. Some of them change their mind and finish inline anyway, and if they do, COMPLETED still arrives and the step can go from deferred to verified. Removing the embed forecloses that.

So the step has three states rather than two:

StateReached byWhat the applicant can do
BlockedNothing attempted yetVerify, or take an escape
DeferredThey asked for the link, or chose manual entryMove on, with the step marked unverified
VerifiedCOMPLETED, with usable data on the orderMove on, with the step marked done

Label the forward action for the state it is in. "Continue" on a verified step and "Continue without verifying here" on a deferred one tell the applicant something true, and they tell your caseworker something true as well.

Only works while the order is open

On a finished order the same call is refused with 400 and "Can't edit the order. Check employers statuses." So wire the button to the order's state, not just to the step: once completed_at is set there is nothing to send them back to.

How hard the manual route is to reach is a setting, not layout

Layout decides what the applicant notices. orders.skip_behavior on the customization template decides whether they can take the manual route immediately or have to attempt a connection first. It is a template setting, not a parameter you send when creating an order:

ValueBehavior
disabledThe product is required. No skip.
anySkip allowed after the applicant tries at least one provider.
allSkip allowed only after the applicant tries every provider on the order.
expireLike any, plus the order auto-completes after auto_complete_after_hours (default 24).

any is the usual public-sector setting: it asks for one honest attempt before the applicant falls back, and it keeps the manual route genuinely available.

Rule 5

Hide the manual link once a connection succeeds.

Don't

Leave Enter manually instead on screen after the applicant has connected.

Do

Remove it the moment a connection is made, along with any manual form you had collapsed behind it.

Why. After a successful connection there is nothing left to enter manually, and leaving it on screen offers a route to replace verified data with a remembered figure. Restore it if the connection fails or the applicant resets the step.

Which event tells you a connection succeeded

onEvent('SUCCESS', payload, 'bridge'). It arrives from the connection widget as soon as the provider returns data, while the applicant is still inside the flow - which is the moment you want.

Do not use onSuccess for this. On an embedded order it fires at the end, after COMPLETED, so a manual link hidden on onSuccess stays visible for the whole session.

onEvent: function (type, payload, source) {
  if (type === 'SUCCESS' && source === 'bridge') hideManualEntry();
  if (type === 'COMPLETED' && source === 'order') enableContinue();
}
Rule 6

Mount inline through the SDK on bridge_token. Never iframe share_url.

Don't

Hand-roll <iframe src="{share_url}">.

Do

Load cdn.truv.com/bridge.js and mount into your own container.

var bridge = TruvBridge.init({
  isOrder: true,                       // required for orders
  bridgeToken: order.bridge_token,
  position: {
    type: 'inline',
    container: document.querySelector('#income-verify')
  }
});

bridge.open();                         // init() alone renders nothing

isOrder: true is required for Embedded Orders. Initializing without it causes unexpected behavior.

Two calls, not one

init() constructs the instance. open() renders it. Call init() alone and you get no frame, no events and no error: the container stays empty, which looks like a bad token or a CSP problem.

Content Security Policy

Missing CSP entries are the other leading cause of a blank frame.

Content-Security-Policy: frame-src my.truv.com; script-src cdn.truv.com;

Those two cover Bridge itself. If you also render employer logos - the logo_url on a company search result, or the company_logo that comes back on the order - those load from a separate Truv CDN host, and a restrictive img-src will block them while Bridge keeps working. Allow the host in the value you are actually served rather than hard-coding one, and ask us to confirm it before you pin it in policy.

Rule 7

Order page inline, connection widget as a modal.

This is the pairing to run inside an application flow. It is two separate settings, at two different layers.

LayerSettingValue
The order page - heading, product choice, Connect / Upload position in TruvBridge.init { type: 'inline', container }
The connection widget the applicant reaches from it Account setting: Suborder launch As a modal

So: the order page lives in your page, and the sign-in step opens as a dialog on top of it. The applicant never leaves your application, and credential entry gets the full viewport it needs.

The deprecated third state is the one that breaks

Suborder launch = Inside the inline frame renders the sign-in within the order frame. It is deprecated as of June 18, 2026. On an account still set that way, a successful payroll connection closes the nested widget and the order page underneath cannot recover. That is the other source of "Unable to proceed."

To tell which mode an account is on, open the sign-in and count the frames:

document.querySelectorAll('iframe').length
// 2 → modern behavior. The suborder has its own parent-level frame.
// 1 → legacy nested mode. Ask Truv to switch the account.

This is an account-level setting. Your TruvBridge.init call does not change.

Full dialog mode is the fallback, not the target

Omit position entirely and Bridge opens as a centered overlay over your page. It works, it needs almost no layout work, and it is the right choice for a caseworker tool or a step you genuinely cannot restructure. Inside an applicant-facing wizard it costs you the continuity that makes the income step feel like part of the application.

Rule 8

Advance on COMPLETED with source: "order".

Don't

Advance, or start polling, on onSuccess or onClose.

Do

Gate on the one event that means the order reached its final state.

onEvent: function (type, payload, source) {
  if (type === 'COMPLETED' && source === 'order') {
    enableContinue();          // the only correct gate
  }
}

Why not onSuccess. On an embedded order it is not the earlier signal it looks like. The documentation describes it as the applicant having connected their accounts and finished on the order page, and in practice it arrives after COMPLETED, not before a connection. So it is no use as a gate and no use as a "they have connected something" trigger either - for that, see Rule 5.

Serve your test page over http, not from a file

Open the page straight off disk on a file:// URL and the embed looks fine - the frame renders, the applicant can connect, the data lands on the order - but SUCCESS and COMPLETED never arrive, and the widget's own Done button stops responding. A file:// page has no real origin for Bridge to post back to, and only the early LOAD events survive it. Anything served from localhost over http behaves normally. Worth knowing before you spend an afternoon on an event handler that was never the problem.

Why not onClose. A close is not necessarily the end of the order. onClose() corresponds to the order-page close, and onEvent('CLOSE', undefined, 'order') fires at the same time. A close originating in the connection widget arrives separately, as onEvent('CLOSE', payload, 'bridge'). So do not treat any CLOSE as abandonment unless source is "order": the connection widget can close while the order page underneath stays live and usable.

It gates verification, not progress

This is the gate for treating the step as verified. It is not the gate for letting the applicant move through the application at all - an applicant who took one of Rule 4's escapes will never reach COMPLETED here, and still has to be able to carry on.

COMPLETED is not the same as verified

COMPLETED fires on the order's final state, and that state can be success or skipped. Reaching it means the applicant is finished with the order, not that usable data came back.

So use it to move the applicant forward, then read the order back on your server and branch on each suborder's own status and last_task_status (employers[] and financial_accounts[] each carry both). The event tells you the applicant is done; the order tells you what you hold.

expired_at is a deadline, not a flag

expired_at is set when the order is created. It is the expiry deadline, not a flag saying the order has expired. The state fields are completed_at, canceled_at and is_expired.

Rule 9

Ask for income once.

Don't

Run Truv and then present the same income in a form the applicant has to fill in or confirm field by field.

Do

Show the verified result as a read-only row with an edit affordance, and let them move on.

If self-certification is on, the applicant has already reviewed every record and confirmed it. A summary screen after that asks them to confirm the same thing twice.

The same applies to document upload. Document upload is part of the order landing page - the Upload documents button beside Connect, with accepted types and labels set per document type on your template. Build a second upload widget elsewhere in your application and you have two paths to the same outcome, one of which produces structured data and one of which does not.

Layout and mounting

Four constraints worth knowing before you hit them.

Set width on the container, never height

New accounts default to automatic frame sizing: the frame grows to fit its content and the page keeps a single scrollbar. Setting a height clips or stretches it. Minimum supported width is 320px - give it more wherever you can.

  • Don't nest the container in a clipped or scroll-trapping parent.
  • If your account is on the legacy fixed height sizing, you must set an explicit height - ideally viewport-relative - and keep the container out of a scrollable parent. Let CSS own that height rather than setting pixel heights from JavaScript on Bridge events.

Have the token before the step renders

Creating the order is a server call, so there is a gap between the applicant arriving at the step and the order page being mountable. Fetch the token as part of loading the step rather than after it paints, and show your own loading state in the container until Bridge is mounted. Otherwise the applicant sees an empty panel where the content should be, which reads as a broken page.

If the call fails, say so in your own words and offer the step again. The applicant cannot act on a Truv error they never see.

Mount after the step has finished laying out

Mounting while a wizard step is still animating open can leave the order page rendering a narrow, mobile-width column inside a full-width container. If you see that, delay the mount until the step has laid out and the container has its final width.

Keep the container stable while Bridge is open

Do not unmount or reparent it. On single-page-app navigation, close Bridge before removing the container. Only one Bridge instance can be open at a time.

Match the template to the order's products

Truv validates the pairing and rejects a mismatch with 400:

{
  "error": {
    "code": "incorrect_parameters",
    "message": "Incorrect request parameters",
    "extra": {
      "invalid-params": [
        { "field": "template_id",
          "message": "Wrong template <id> for order products" }
      ]
    }
  }
}

A template bound to ["income","assets"] cannot be used with an income-only order, and vice versa. If you need both an income-only and an income-plus-assets flow, you need two templates.

Related: products: ["income"] already includes employment data. Do not pass ["income","employment"].

Pre-launch QA

Walk this on a real device, in a real browser, on the deployed environment. Several of these fail only when hosted.

Layout and mounting

  • Income step renders the landing page on arrival, with no click in between
  • Nothing above the embed except your own application chrome
  • Frame reaches full width at 1440px and does not render a mobile column
  • Single page scrollbar - no scroll trapped inside the frame
  • Usable at 375px wide, and on a short viewport
  • Self-certification screen scrolls far enough to reach its submit button

Flow

  • Sign-in opens as a modal over the order page, not inside it - frame count is 2
  • Manual link sits below the embed, with the send-link button beside it
  • Manual link disappears once a connection succeeds
  • Dismissing the connection modal does not show an abandonment state
  • Continue becomes active only on COMPLETED / source: "order"
  • If the template has self-certification on, the review step appears in the flow and submitting there ends the Truv leg
  • Completion lands on the next real section, with the income step ticked
  • Returning to the income step shows a confirmation, not a second embed
  • Leave mid-connection, reload the step: the order reinitializes on the same bridge_token and the earlier connection is still listed
  • A completed order does not reinitialize - the step shows the verified result
  • An expired order produces a new order on the same external_user_id, and its landing page lists the earlier connections
  • bridge.open() is called - init() on its own renders nothing and throws no error

Data and identity

  • One order per adult - unique external_user_id, shared order_number
  • A household of three produces three distinct Truv users
  • task-status-updated webhooks received, and the X-WEBHOOK-SIGN header verified against the raw request body rather than re-serialized JSON
  • Order read-back shows the products you requested
  • certification=certified returns what your eligibility file expects
  • Where you hold an employer name, it is on the order: the order page lists that employer and Connect goes straight to the provider sign-in, with no search screen
  • An employer name Truv cannot match still creates the order, and the applicant lands on the employer search screen

Failure paths

  • Wrong credentials, then recovery
  • An unsupported employer, then the manual fallback
  • Applicant closes the browser mid-flow, returns via the emailed link, finishes
  • An expired link renders your expired-screen copy

Build order

Each step is testable before the next one matters, which keeps the integration reviewable as it goes.

  1. Settings first. Confirm the account-level pairing and the template values in the configuration checklist. Several cannot be worked around in code, so finding them after the build is the expensive order.
  2. Create and read an order server-side. One order per adult, your own id in external_user_id, your case number in order_number. Prove you can read it back and see the suborders.
  3. Mount it on one step. SDK, inline, on arrival. At this point the applicant can complete a verification end to end.
  4. Gate the step. Advance only on COMPLETED with source: "order", then read the order and branch on each suborder.
  5. Build the two escapes. The manual link and the send-link button, below the embed.
  6. Handle the return visit. Reinitialize an open order, create a new one otherwise.
  7. Deeplink. Pass the employer wherever you already hold one.
  8. Walk the pre-launch checks on the deployed environment, on a real device.

What needs Truv

Suborder launch and frame sizing are account-level: we set them, you cannot. Ask for suborder launch as a modal and automatic frame sizing before you start building, because the first one in particular will look like a bug in your own code. For anything else, send an order id with the question - it gets answered faster than a description does.
